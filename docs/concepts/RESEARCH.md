# Sponsor package concept research

_Checked August 22, 2026._

## Freedom Lab story

Freedom Lab’s current site frames the organization around learning, teaching, and developing practical freedom technology, and explicitly names Bitcoin, encrypted messaging, community internet, decentralized social media, open-source AI, self-hosting, and open-source hardware.[1]

The longer “started / now / difference / next” narrative in the concepts comes from Harrison’s supplied brief rather than an outside source. The two new story images are the exact supplied files; neither was regenerated or restyled.

For the selected Signal Rings direction, the “Where we are” photo uses `src/assets/community-01.jpg` at its original local resolution of 1242×864. OpenCV feature matching confirmed it is the same underlying photograph as Harrison’s attached 613×369 crop while preserving the fuller frame.

## Community rings

The audited local Freedom Lab dashboard snapshot contained 841 embedded person records; the dashboard’s canonical identity-merging logic reduced those to 830 displayed people. Of those, 624 had at least one approved RSVP, 196 had two or more, 45 had four or more, and 15 had six to thirteen.[11]

These are RSVP-history figures, not physical check-in counts. The concepts label that distinction directly on the momentum page.

The selected deck consolidates the profile and RSVP rows into Harrison’s approved `830+ Total attendees` line, followed by 195+, 45+, and 15+. The audited 624 approved-RSVP count remains in this research record and the dashboard source.

Across the 29 dashboard-tracked events, the headline event totals sum to 1,055 approved/going RSVPs plus 116 Luma waitlist records, or 1,171 combined. The two older manually curated 2024 archive entries do not expose Luma RSVP totals in the dashboard dataset.[11]

The four smaller statistic cards now read `15+` core members, `800+` calendar subscribers, `45+` attendees per event, and `30+` events hosted. These follow the same conservative rounding convention as the audience shelf.

## Event archive

Freedom Lab’s public Classes and Events page links the live Luma calendar and exposes the site’s local event archive.[2]

The public archive contained 30 past events.[3]

The Luma future feed contained one upcoming event, “Agentic Payments and the Future of Sovereign Money,” scheduled for September 1, 2026.[4]

The two sources were deduplicated into a 31-event local manifest, ordered from October 27, 2024 through September 1, 2026.[3][4]

Every archive card uses the exact source title, date, event status, and source cover artwork. The category summary is 19 Bitcoin, 6 Sovereign AI, and 6 Freedom Lab General; two 2024 Bitcoin events absent from the dashboard category dataset were classified by their explicit Bitcoin/Lightning titles.

## Featured guests

Seth for Privacy’s official site describes his work as education at the intersection of technology and privacy and documents the Opt Out podcast plus public privacy, Bitcoin, and Monero infrastructure.[5] His public GitHub profile identifies him as Cake Wallet’s COO and a privacy educator.[6] The deck uses his official profile avatar rather than seeking or exposing a face photo for a pseudonymous privacy educator.[6]

The Free Software Foundation describes itself as a nonprofit with a worldwide mission to promote computer user freedom, sponsor the GNU Project, publish the GNU GPL and related licenses, and defend free-software licensing through its compliance work.[7] Because the featured guest is an institution, the deck uses the official FSF logo rather than inventing a representative person.[7]

Frank Corva’s official Forbes profile describes him as a contributor covering Bitcoin adoption around the world, especially in developing countries, host of *new renaissance capital*, and former White House correspondent for Bitcoin Magazine.[8] The deck uses his official Forbes contributor portrait.[8]

Human Rights Foundation identifies Win Ko Ko Aung as a global Bitcoin adoption fellow who helps nonprofits operating under authoritarian regimes use Bitcoin for financial freedom.[9] HRF reports that his 2025 Burma aid campaign raised more than five million sats, funded tens of thousands of meals, and delivered rice to 350 households.[9] Bitcoin Policy Institute documents his earlier Human Rights Fellowship and provides the official portrait used in the deck.[10]

## Visual directions

- **Signal Rings** — cinematic dark-green photo spreads, concentric audience signals, 2×2 guest cards, and a 4×4 visual event mosaic.
- **Field Notes** — light editorial paper, a documentary timeline, horizontal guest profiles, and a two-column archive ledger.
- **Street Level** — architectural overlays, a building-centered story, vertical guest “floors,” and façade-like event windows.
- **Open Protocol** — modular story records, network orbits, technical guest nodes, and terminal-style archive rows.

Each option contains the same exact story and evidence. Signal Rings now uses five pages by integrating featured guests into Classes & Events and fitting all 31 events into a half-page square-image grid with full event titles and category-color lines along the bottom edge of each card; the other studies remain seven pages. The compositions differ materially; they are not palette swaps.
